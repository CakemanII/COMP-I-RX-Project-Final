Shoreline barriers

  • Shoreline barriers mainly consist of plants and rocks placed along the shoreline to capture unwanted contaminants.
  • Rocks can also be deeper in the water away from the shore to help break up powerful waves and catch debre before reaching the land.

Mangrove Natural Barriers

  • Mangrove natural barriers reduce wave energy, limit erosion, and support many aquatic and terrestrial species.
  • Their dense root systems trap sediment, stabilize the surrounding environment, and reduce land loss.

Backyard Natural Barriers

  • Many natural barriers can actually be found embeded within our community.
  • Creating natural barriers such as the water irregator as shown, can help prevent water from accumulating and prevent flooding.

Submerged Natural Barriers

  • A majority of natural barriers are located below the water’s surface, where they protect shorelines and support marine ecosystems.
  • Additionally, these also capture debre, provide habitats for organisms living in the water.
