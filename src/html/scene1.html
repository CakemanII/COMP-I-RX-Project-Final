Flooding

  • Flooding occurs when water overflows onto normally dry land due to storms, high tides, or rising sea levels.
  • Plants can perish and habitats start to disappear when ecosystems like marshes or seagrass beds are submerged for extended periods of time.
  • Due to rapidly changing environments (primarily from human interference and development), flooding is becoming increasingly frequent.

Storm Surge

  • Storm surge occurs when hurricanes produce strong winds that force large amounts of ocean water onto land.
  • This can significantly increasing water levels on costal communities and ecosystems, such as many with in Tampa Bay.
  • Because of Tampa Bay's shallow, funnel-shaped geometry, which traps incoming water and exacerbates flooding during storms, storm surge is one of the biggest threats.

Climate Change

  • Sea levels are rising and natural weather patterns are being disturbed as a result of climate change's warming of the ocean and atmosphere.
  • Warmer oceans supply more energy to storms, making power systems such as hurricanes stronger and more destructive.

Hurricanes

  • Due to climate change, hurricanes are becoming more and more powerful.
  • Stronger hurricanes can produce greater storm surge, heavier rainfall, and more severe flooding that damages coastal habitats as well as inland habitats.
  • Scientists have observed that modern hurricanes tend to strengthen faster and last longer, increasing the risk to Tampa Bay’s ecosystems.
